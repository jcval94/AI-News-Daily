# Recording Pack + Teleprompter

The recording layer converts an approved essay into a presenter-facing capture package without modifying editorial content.

For every approved episode it emits:

- `recording_pack.json` — machine-readable take contract;
- `camera_script.md` — human-readable recording sheet;
- `teleprompter.html` — standalone offline teleprompter with no external dependencies.

## Core rule

`script.txt` remains editorial authority. The recording pack may split it into takes, but it must reconstruct the approved narration exactly after whitespace normalization.

A CTA generated by `production_script.json` is carried as a separate `cta` take and never silently merged into the approved essay.

## Take strategy

Default policy:

- minimum target: 20 seconds;
- preferred target: 35 seconds;
- maximum target: 60 seconds;
- split first at sentence boundaries;
- use clause/word fallback only for unusually long sentences.

Take IDs are stable and machine-friendly, for example `opening_t01`, `b02_evidence_t01`, and `synthesis_t01`.

Speaking the slate `TAKE <take_id>` is recommended but not mandatory. It creates an inexpensive anchor for later ingest/alignment.

## Teleprompter

The HTML is self-contained and can be opened directly from disk. It supports:

- take-by-take navigation;
- 3-second countdown;
- start/pause auto-scroll;
- font size and scroll-speed controls;
- mirror mode;
- fullscreen;
- optional director/edit notes;
- keyboard shortcuts;
- local persistence for font, speed and mirror preferences.

Director notes are hidden by default so they do not contaminate delivery.

## Relationship to edit_manifest

When `edit_manifest.json` exists, overlapping media cues are attached to each take as context. These remain editing suggestions; they are never inserted into spoken narration.

## Capture defaults

4K/30 fps, 16:9 and 48 kHz audio are recommendations, not hard requirements. The contract remains editor/camera neutral.


## Style traceability

The Recording Pack inherits the editing-style metadata from `edit_manifest.json`. This lets recording, virtual A-roll, and later NLE timelines prove which audiovisual grammar was active without reading mutable repository state.
